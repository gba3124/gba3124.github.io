Privacy Policy

Scoopdown does not collect, store, transmit, or sell any personal data.

Last updated: 2026-10-12.

It has no analytics, no tracking, no accounts and no servers. Everything it does happens in your browser, and what it makes (Markdown, CSV, text) goes only to your clipboard or your Downloads folder.

What runs where

  • On every web page, a small script waits for the pointer to rest on a table, so the table button can appear there. Until then it only checks whether the element under the pointer is a table, and remembers the element you right-click so "this table" and "this link" know what you meant. It sends nothing anywhere.
  • When you use Scoopdown (the toolbar button, a shortcut, a right-click item or the table button), it reads that page, the table, your selection or the video's captions, builds the result in your browser, and keeps nothing afterward.
  • Tabs → one AI context reads the content of the tabs you tick, in your browser, to combine them into one paste.

Permissions

  • activeTab, scripting: run the grab on the page you are on when you ask for it (including pages opened before Scoopdown was installed).
  • Content script on http://*/* and https://*/*: the table button and the right-click items above. It has to be on the page before you point at a table, and a table can be on any site.
  • contextMenus: adds "Copy page / selection / link / this table" to the right-click menu.
  • tabs: reads the titles and addresses of your open tabs for the Tabs list, and finds the page the panel should read.
  • clipboardWrite: puts what you asked for on your clipboard. A shortcut, a right-click item or the table button copies on your behalf, sometimes seconds after your last click on the page, and the browser only lets an extension write the clipboard then with this permission. Scoopdown never reads your clipboard.
  • storage: remembers your settings (synced by your browser to your own account), the panel's position, the catalog number of your last copy, and a counter for a one-time review request.

Network activity

Scoopdown sends nothing to the developer or anyone else. Getting a transcript opens the site's own transcript panel (YouTube) or loads the video's own caption file (other sites); your browser fetches those from that site exactly as it would if you turned captions on yourself.
